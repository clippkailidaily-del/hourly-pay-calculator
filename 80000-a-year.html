$80,000 a Year Is How Much an Hour?

The quick answer, the full breakdown, and how it compares with hourly jobs.

$80,000 a year = per hour (40 hrs/week, 52 weeks)$38.46

$80,000 a year is about $38.46 an hour if you work full time, 40 hours a week for 52 weeks, before taxes. The math is the standard conversion in reverse: $80,000 ÷ 2,080 working hours = $38.46 an hour. This is the number to use when you compare a salaried offer with an hourly-paid job.

$80,000 a year after taxes: how much do you actually take home?

What kind of jobs pay around $80,000 a year?

Office-based professional work fills this band. Business and financial operations occupations had a median of $39.74 an hour in May 2025, $82,660 a year, and life, physical and social science occupations a median of $39.68 an hour, about $82,530 a year, per the Bureau of Labor Statistics.

One occupation lands almost exactly on this salary: claims adjusters, examiners and investigators, at a $37.50 hourly median, $78,000 a year at full-time hours.

Is $80,000 a year a good salary?

For scale: at a standard schedule it equals $38.46 an hour, which is about 5.3× the US federal minimum wage of $7.25 an hour, and it works out to $6,666.67 a month before tax. Whether it is good for you depends mainly on your area's housing costs and the benefits package, paid holidays, health insurance and a 401(k) match can add 20–40% on top of a salary. That puts $80,000 some 24.9% above the BLS median full-time earnings of $64,064 a year ($1,232 a week in the third quarter of 2026).

Gross, not take-home: all figures here are before federal and state income tax, Social Security and Medicare. Your actual paychecks will be lower.

How do I convert any salary to hourly?

Divide the annual salary by the hours you work in a year. For a standard full-time schedule that is salary ÷ 2,080. The calculator does it for any salary, hours and weeks.

About $5,425.83 a month take-home, roughly $65,110 a year, for a single filer after federal income tax and Social Security/Medicare, before any state tax. See the after-tax breakdown above for the full estimate.
