$24 an Hour Is How Much a Year?

The quick answer, the full breakdown, and what it really means for your paycheck.

$24 an hour = per year (40 hrs/week, 52 weeks)$49,920

$24 an hour is $49,920 a year if you work full time, 40 hours a week for 52 weeks, before taxes. That is the standard conversion employers and payroll departments use: $24 × 2,080 working hours = $49,920. Use it as the benchmark whenever you compare an hourly offer with a salaried one.

What if you take unpaid time off?

The $49,920 headline assumes all 52 weeks are paid. Hourly workers without paid vacation earn less: at 50 paid weeks (two weeks off unpaid), $24 an hour comes to $48,000 a year; at 48 weeks it is $46,080. Salaried jobs at the same headline figure usually keep paying during holidays and vacation, which is one reason a salary of $49,920 is often worth a little more than $24 an hour with no paid time off.

$24 an hour after taxes: how much do you actually take home?

The figures above are gross pay. As a single filer in 2026, a full-time worker at $24 an hour would keep roughly $42,291 a year, about $3,524.23 a month after federal income tax and Social Security/Medicare (FICA). Here is the estimate, line by line:

Estimate only: single filer, 2026 federal tax brackets with the $16,100 standard deduction, FICA at 7.65%. State income tax is not included (it ranges from 0% in states like Texas and Florida to over 10% in high-tax states), and 401(k) contributions, health insurance premiums or credits would change the result. Your real paychecks will vary, treat this as a planning figure, not tax advice.

Jobs that commonly pay around $24 an hour

Plenty of skilled hands-on and office jobs sit right around this rate. General maintenance and repair workers, the people who keep buildings and equipment running, had a median wage of $23.84 an hour in May 2025, according to the Bureau of Labor Statistics. Bookkeeping, accounting and auditing clerks were at $24.36, medical records specialists at $24.59, and welders, cutters, solderers and brazers at $25.84.

In other words, $24 an hour is typical pay for experienced trades and back-office work rather than entry-level jobs, most roles at this level ask for proven skills or a certification.

Is $24 an hour a good wage?

It depends on where you live and what the job includes, but for scale: the US federal minimum wage is $7.25 an hour, so $24 an hour is about 3.3× the federal minimum. A full-time gross of $49,920 works out to $4,160 a month before tax. Whether that feels comfortable depends mostly on housing costs in your area and whether the job adds benefits, health insurance and a 401(k) match can add 20–40% on top of the wage. Compare any offer on this page's hourly to salary calculator so both sides are in the same units. For a national yardstick, a full-time year at $24 an hour ($49,920) is 22.1% below the median full-time earnings of $64,064 a year, the median full-time worker earned $1,232 a week in the third quarter of 2026, according to the BLS.

Gross, not take-home: all figures here are before federal and state income tax, Social Security and Medicare. Your actual paychecks will be lower.

Frequently asked questions

How much is $24 an hour per year?

What is $24 an hour biweekly?

How much is $24 an hour with overtime?
